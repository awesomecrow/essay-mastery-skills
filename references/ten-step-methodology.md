# The 10-Step Iterative Essay Writing Methodology

The classical 10-step iterative framework separates the act of **production** from the act of **editing**, preventing premature self-censorship while guaranteeing uncompromising final density.

---

## Phase 1: Inception
### Step 1: Topic Question Formulation
Formulate the topic not as a passive category ("A Study on Free Will"), but as an explicit, high-tension interrogative ("Does determinism eliminate moral responsibility in legal jurisprudence?").
* **The Fascination & Non-Boredom Rule:** If you are bored while writing it, the reader will be paralyzed with boredom while reading it. Select a question whose answer genuinely matters to you.

### Step 2: Source Curation & Gist Synthesis
Read primary sources. Close the books or articles. Write down 3-5 key points completely from memory (*Gist Synthesis*). Extracting notes from memory forces cognitive assimilation and prevents unconscious plagiarism.

---

## Phase 2: Architecture
### Step 3: Primary Outline (The Spine)
Construct a sequence of 10 to 15 core sentences.
* **The 1:100 Rule:** Produce roughly 1 structural outline sentence per ~100 words of final target length (e.g., a 1,200-word essay requires 12 outline sentences).
* **No Vacuous Placeholders:** Prohibit meta-commentary like *"In this paragraph I will introduce my thesis"*. State the proposition directly: *"Technological acceleration destabilizes institutional memory by shortening generational feedback loops"*.

---

## Phase 3: Generation
### Step 4: Rough Draft Expansion (+25% Buffer Target)
Expand each outline sentence into a full paragraph.
* **The Buffer Expansion Target:** Aim for $+20\%\text{ to }+30\%$ above the target final word count. For a 1,500-word essay, produce ~1,875 words.
* **Production / Editing Decoupling:** Write continuously without self-censoring. You cannot sculpt without sufficient marble; excess raw prose provides the dispensable scaffolding needed for rigorous pruning.

---

## Phase 4: Multi-Pass Editing
### Step 5: Dual-Sentence Rewrite (15%–25% Trimming)
Take every paragraph line by line. Underneath every sentence of the rough draft, write a leaner, punchier rewrite.
* **Trimming Metric:** Aim for an overall word count reduction of $15\%\text{ to }25\%$.
* **Focus:** Eliminate passive hedges, convert nominalizations back to active verbs, and select precise terminology.

### Step 6: Sentence Sequencing within Paragraphs
Examine the sentences within each paragraph. Does Sentence 3 presuppose information not delivered until Sentence 5? Reorder sentences until internal logic flows smoothly.

### Step 7: Paragraph Re-ordering & Logical Progression
Review the entire sequence of paragraphs. Verify that each paragraph acts as an essential stepping stone to the next.

---

## Phase 5: Distillation
### Step 8: Memory-Distillation Re-Outlining
Internalize the entire document. Put it aside. Synthesize a new 10–12 sentence outline purely from memory.
* Compare this memory-distilled outline with your draft: any digressions that vanished from your memory were likely superficial flab and can be safely purged.

### Step 9: Iterative Convergence
Reassemble the revised draft. Read it aloud. Continue refining until successive reading passes yield zero delta in word efficiency and rhythm.

---

## Phase 6: Verification
### Step 10: Citation Integrity & Formatting
Verify every factual claim, borrowed statistic, and direct quotation against standard academic style guides (APA, MLA, or Chicago). Assemble the terminal References section.
