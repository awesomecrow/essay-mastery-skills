# Template: Philosophical & Dialectical Argument

> **Target Standard:** 10–15 paragraph stepping-stones, ~1,200–1,500 words.  
> **Philosophy:** A true essay is an act of discovery, testing a proposition against its strongest counterargument.

---

## 1. The Core Interrogative
State the fundamental question clearly. Avoid survey prompts ("A discussion on...").

## 2. Thesis & Primary Proposition
What is your definitive answer to the question? State it without hedges.

## 3. Foundational Premises (Paragraphs 1–3)
- Premise 1: Foundational definition and scope.
- Premise 2: The conceptual mechanism or logical link.
- Premise 3: Concrete demonstration / historical illustration.

## 4. The Antagonist / Strongest Counterargument (Paragraphs 4–6)
- What is the most formidable objection a brilliant critic could raise?
- Why does that objection appear persuasive on the surface?

## 5. Dialectical Synthesis & Refutation (Paragraphs 7–9)
- Where does the counterargument fail or rely on unexamined assumptions?
- How does surviving this challenge deepen the primary thesis?

## 6. Higher-Order Implications & Conclusion (Paragraphs 10–12)
- What does this truth demand of the reader or of disciplinary understanding?
- Close with a clear, resonant terminal proposition.
